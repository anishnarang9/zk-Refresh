## Paper methodology versus this product

Paper §5 pp23–26 uses BLS12-381 on AWS c7i.48xlarge with 192 vCPUs. Setup/preprocessing are averages of three executions; other numbers average ten. Random R1CS instances use assignment length equal to constraint count (`m=n` in the paper). Controlled updates set `|ΔZ|=2d`, `|ΔA|=|ΔB|=d`; footnote 4 warns setting all three equal would not allow meaningful R1CS witness updates. Table 2 explicitly handles unequal supports. Do not translate a single revoked leaf into `d=1`.

E2 instead uses the fixed BN254 credential R1CS: 648,156 constraints, 643,563 assignment coordinates including one, five public signals and padded N=1,048,576. A near sibling change affects 5,081 assignment coordinates. Paper §3.1 defines d as assignment Hamming distance |ΔZ|; its sparse-R1CS efficiency shorthand assumes row supports |ΔA|, |ΔB|, |ΔC|=O(d), while the algorithms still work without that assumption. Table 2 separates assignment and nonzero A/B row-evaluation counts. None means tree leaves, tree height or all touched entries. A/B column traversal can touch a row that sums to zero; current sparse merge retains such zeros. Record both the actual implementation's retained row count and the mathematical nonzero support. C-row differences are relevant satisfaction diagnostics but not the paper's cross-term update-time parameter (§5.1).

Paper preprocessing at n=2^20 is 74.3 s with full parallelization; serial 5,158 s is an estimate extrapolated beyond measured n=2^18. Its 59.4 s no-trapdoor derivation is distinct from 1.535 s Groth16 setup with trapdoor. None can be compared directly with this four-thread BN254 workstation's 382.969/510.323 s public/private stages. They do establish that substantial group preprocessing is intrinsic to the presented approach and must be included in total workload economics.

Paper §5.1 p25 explicitly benchmarks naive O(d²) field cross terms because these are faster than sparse convolution for evaluated sizes, with group operations dominating those examples. Retaining this choice is not evidence that the authors failed to implement their evaluated scheme. The faster asymptotic option remains an important roadmap capability/experiment, but its concrete crossover must be measured under our unequal supports, conversions, fields, threading and allocation costs. Paper Fig.3 crossover thresholds are neither BN254 guarantees nor product mode-selection thresholds.

The paper evaluates proof update operations; it supplies no evidence for X.509 DER compatibility, legitimate enterprise signer access, Solana timing, root churn, action cache lifetime, witness refresh, abandonment or platform fit. Its unchanged ordinary proof shape supports compatibility goals, not our original-zkey reduction mapping or transaction encoding. E2 establishes a fair supplied-witness anchored comparison and direct local refresh benefit; it does not close these external workload questions.

## Product workload hypotheses and rejection criteria

The accepted product direction is coherent, but its DYNARK economic advantage remains a hypothesis. A permissioned work request is naturally a finite action: a holder may cancel, complete after zero retries, lose eligibility, or submit a new request whose signature changes 104,175 compiled values in the historical census. Credential lifetime is not usable same-action cache lifetime.

E2's mean full/update saving is 1.885 s per frozen comparable refresh, while private preparation is 510.323 s. Its ideal shared-public/no-I/O crossover of 271 refreshes is a best-case accounting scenario, not observed behavior; conservative mixed-I/O/public scenarios are 312/515. For one to five retries, measured private preparation would need roughly 270× to 54× reduction respectively at that fixed saving to pay back (arithmetic illustration, not feasibility claim). Decreasing witness latency changes the comparison and must be measured in the future complete path. Sharing public keys does not share holder/action-private projections.

Plausible populations/depths, source cadence, batches, submission delays, root events during proving and actual per-action reuse all need consenting-issuer/pilot evidence. Synthetic Poisson or burst traces can establish controlled failure regions, but cannot certify prevalence. Distinguish freshness heartbeats with no root change, issuance that really changes a root, issuer disable and own-revocation. Fail-closed status policy may pause execution without yielding a useful proof-refresh workload. Delaying effective revocation to manufacture retries/savings violates accepted semantics.

Default full proving for short/unknown sessions is consistent with current evidence. Completing the reusable DYNARK engine can still meet the research objective even if this pilot seldom selects cached proving. A product efficiency claim is rejected unless actual action-lifecycle total paid work—including preparation, failed attempts, eviction/reload, background/reconstruction and new actions—beats the matching full path under equal authorization guarantees.
