The core's intended scope is a supported fixed R1CS over BN254 with one exact Groth16 setup, admitted reduction/domain/query encoding and valid assignments. Public-input names and counts are descriptor data, rather than credential constants inside proof arithmetic. Tests vary assignment counts above and below padded N, constraint counts before padding, public/private splits, sparse/dense induced changes, C-only public dependence and matching-key Original versus instance-augmented reductions. Unsupported counts, fields, indices, domains or configured work/memory bounds return typed failure. These observations support engine portability across the tested fixed-relation regimes; they do not establish arbitrary circuit mutation, other curves, every Groth16 setup or device-wide resource bounds.

The actual credential workload is a separate narrow experiment: unchanged synthetic 439-byte TBS profile, two RSA-2048/e65537/SHA-256 checks, depth-10 tree, m=648156, v=643563, six instance coordinates and N=1048576. Near/middle/far fixtures have different before states; their support counts, preparation costs and future measured refresh times are workload-specific. Repeated latency samples of one fixture are not distinct-root churn observations. The existing serial trace has eleven valid states, not a measured 100-update credential workload. Synthetic 100-update engine traces must retain that distinction.

Security claims stay within the corrected [paper analysis](../phase-1/evidence/paper-security.md) and [conformance record](conformance.md): fixed relation, valid base/target assignments, exact setup/reduction, fresh independent caller randomizers and the stated algebraic/auxiliary-input assumptions. Pairing acceptance establishes relation acceptance under those assumptions; it does not establish a new zero-knowledge proof, adaptive simulation/extraction, cache-compromise security or side-channel resistance. Private projections, assignments and retained blinders remain sensitive. No general secure-erasure claim follows from best-effort zeroization or filesystem permissions.

Internal snapshots and authenticated local revisions never replace execution-current-root verification, exact action/replay binding or fresh authenticated issuer status. Repeating an old root with a new timestamp cannot refresh issuer status. Own revocation, issuer disablement and action completion require local invalidation hooks now and qualified external authority integration later. A held local lease can serialize local invalidation; it does not implement distributed instantaneous revocation. Media durability, durable trusted generations/key service, real credential parsing, qualified OIDs/DER bounds, incremental witness generation, original-key adaptation and Solana/application execution remain outside the established Phase 2 evidence.
