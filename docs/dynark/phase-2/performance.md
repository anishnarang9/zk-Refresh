## Narrow allocation hypothesis

Fresh source inspection finds pinned `ark-bn254 0.5.0`, `curves/g1.rs:43–48`, sends projective multiplication to `GLVConfig::glv_mul_projective`. Pinned `ark-ec 0.5.0`, `scalar_mul/glv.rs:25–86`, decomposes each scalar through allocating BigInt arithmetic. The already-retained E4 first preparation sample contains these decomposition/conversion/allocation paths under group work. This establishes an executed code path, not the fraction of total allocations or elapsed time it causes.

Candidate: retain exact signed GLV decompositions for repeatedly used **public FFT twiddles**, generated by the unchanged pinned decomposition; reuse them in the same endomorphism/joint multiplication. The equation is k=k1+lambda*k2 modulo the scalar order, so [k]P=[k1]P+[k2]phi(P), with the original signs. This is a representation/reuse hypothesis, not a new cryptographic algorithm. It needs high-reasoning review, independent multiplication equality including zero/one/order-boundary scalars and exceptional points, exact transform/projection equality and a microbenchmark that charges cache construction/retention. Do not cache witness-dependent scalar decompositions in a public package. A multiplication loop rewrite must not accidentally alter scalar signs, bit traversal, subgroup assumptions or side-channel claims.

Reject this candidate if retained public memory/load outweighs allocation/time savings or if it complicates the bounded scheduler without measured benefit. A lower allocation count alone is not a speed claim. Dependency source remains pinned; no global Cargo registry patch is allowed. Public preparation, private preparation and full proving must each state whether they use the candidate, so a backend improvement is not attributed uniquely to DYNARK.

### P2-E3a observed result

The [isolated experiment](../../../results/dynark/phase-2/representation/glv/report.md) is complete. Parent reviewed the exact multiplication recipe, read raw diagnostic/timing samples and aggregate statistics, confirmed executable/source hashes, and independently reran all three equality tests (passed in 1.64 seconds). Tests cover all forward/inverse twiddle entries through N=4096, normalization and boundary/exceptional/rescaled points. The product backend remains unchanged.

Thirty counterbalanced measured batches after two excluded warm-ups at each of N=256 and N=4096 show a negative paid-work result for 65 sampled multipliers repeated once or four times. Table construction plus multiplication plus destruction lost in every measured pair. At N=256, four repeats took median 7.643 ms pinned versus 8.089 ms paid cached; at N=4096, 7.464 versus 15.776 ms. Warm cached multiplication was modestly lower, but these short batches do not establish a precise speed percentage or a full FFT benefit.

Separate allocation diagnostics observed zero cached-kernel allocation calls, versus 8,468 calls/258,064 requested traffic bytes for four pinned repeats. Construction itself incurred 16,902/270,342 calls for N=256/4096 and retained 41,040/655,440 requested bytes. The entire public table is charged despite only 65 sampled multipliers. Requested heap, sampled RSS and logical table payload are reported separately; the very short N=256 diagnostic RSS sample is underresolved. This rejects admission for the tested low-reuse workloads. Higher reuse remains an unmeasured hypothesis; no product integration or complete preparation/session gain is approved by these observations.

### P2-E4b observed cross-kernel result

The [frozen synthetic experiment](../../../results/dynark/phase-2/cross-term/synthetic-timing-report.md) completed exact coefficient and separate group comparisons within its finite budget. On 30 counterbalanced uninstrumented pairs per primary case, Fast lost every comparison for support unions of at least two. Four intermediate sizes had only two exploratory pairs each and also lost. The separate diagnostic observed 517,072,496 requested traffic bytes, 785,174 allocation calls and 8,122,696 additional peak live requested bytes for near Fast; remainder evaluation occupied 90.9% of the observed whole call. Combining coefficients reduced synthetic group calls for overlapping supports, but the near/middle/far disjoint cases had equal calls and nearly equal group time; no complete-update gain follows. Actual credential supports and total-session measurements remain open. The public synthetic tables and group bases are not a validated credential package or private preparation result.

## Selective dependency hypothesis for T7 review

A requested projection entry `q_i^A=sum_j a_j phi_ij` depends on the entire nonzero anchor A vector, not only coordinate i. An on-demand direct calculation can avoid retaining both full N-entry vectors, but costs dense anchor traversal and group work per requested entry; sparse requested output alone does not imply cheaper preparation. A bounded selective cache may compute a few requested entries and otherwise choose validated full proving. Cache identity includes exact anchor/setup/action and requested rows. No cross-action reuse is approved.

Falsifiable objective: lower total session work and retained private bytes for explicitly sparse-demand workloads, including cold misses and abandoned preparation, while returning exactly the eager projection values. Compare against full proving and eager anchors over 0/1/2/5/10/100+ updates. Accepted small tests check exact entries and raw components against eager and independent polynomial oracles. Finite requested-row/group-work ceilings prevent a near/new-action workload from triggering thousands of dense calculations. An evaluated loss is an acceptable T7 finding. The [high-reasoning dependency review](evidence/t7-dependency.md), [accepted source/tests](../../../results/dynark/phase-2/selective/parent-acceptance.md) and parent formula review support the bounded implementation; a separately reviewed session experiment still precedes performance claims.
